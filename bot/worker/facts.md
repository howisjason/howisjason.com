## What he fixes (the four problems on his page)

1. Leads that wait. The inquiry that sat for a day and went to whoever answered first; the
   quote nobody chased. Fix: every lead gets a reply drafted in minutes, ready for the
   owner's OK, and a follow-up on time, without anyone having to remember.
2. Admin that eats the day. The same emails, quotes and updates typed out by hand, and the
   late nights catching up. Fix: the repeat work moves to a simple system the team can
   actually run.
3. Answers buried in your software. You pay for software that holds the answers, but
   nobody has time to dig them out. Fix: he connects the software you already have so the
   answer comes to you. Only then would he suggest anything new.
4. Customers who never came back. Years of past customers in a list, or on paper, that
   nobody contacts. Fix: they hear from you again, personally. A customer who already
   trusts you is the easiest one to win back.

One inquiry, step by step (the example on his page): a new inquiry comes in ("Hi, can you
quote a kitchen tap replacement?"), a reply is drafted in minutes, the owner taps OK,
follow-ups go out on day 2 and day 5, and the job is booked.

## How he works (his four rules)

- One piece at a time. Start with the thing costing you the most, see it work, then decide
  what's next.
- A person says yes. Nothing reaches a customer without your OK, and AI is kept out of the
  decisions where one mistake costs you a customer.
- Nothing new to learn. It works inside the software your team already uses, unless
  something new is clearly worth it.
- A real person answers. You talk to Jason himself, in plain English.

What a business gets: fewer things slipping through the cracks (missed follow-ups, lost
leads, repeat admin); simple systems the team can run, explained in plain English; AI
where it pays off, kept out of decisions where one wrong call costs a customer.

   - When his emails were landing in spam, he found the one sentence setting off the
     filters; a later test reached 19 of 20 inboxes.

     personal message, written the way you would write it, and a follow-up on time, so
     none go cold.

     own words.
2. An assistant that runs his admin every morning.

     asks Jason when a person is needed. 450+ pieces of its work have been graded so far;
